  <h1 class="font-weight-bold title">Nobody investigates a number that's going up.</h1>

Our mailable audience grew beautifully, right up until it blew past our account limit and tripped an alarm. I got handed the question. The integration wasn't matching on email address, and the duplicates turned out to be the start of a long journey.
  </p>

  <p>

  </p>

  <p>

My favorite work sits where the CRM and the marketing platform disagree and someone has to determine which system is lying.

  <h2>Some of my favorite challenges have been:</h2>

  <h3>The CRM stopped receiving qualified leads from MCAE</h3>
  <p>
    <strong>Action:</strong> I took ownership and traced the failure across four source systems. I discovered that nearly all incoming prospect records were missing a single field that blocked qualification entirely. I collaborated with the Dev team to close the data gap, designed a research pipeline with Sales leadership, and got sign-off from Business on the new qualification process.
  </p>
  <p>
    <strong>Result:</strong> The inside sales team received leads again and everyone was happy.
  </p>

  <h3>A new business unit's dedicated IP address needed to be warmed during an active promotion season</h3>
  <p>
    <strong>Action:</strong> I designed an eight-stage IP warming program using engagement-tiered audience expansion with provider-specific tracks, defined growth rates per stage, and a tapering cadence. I tracked performance in MCAE, Everest, and Google Postmaster Tools, and I set a decision checkpoint to fall back to a secondary sending platform rather than relying on open-ended monitoring.
  </p>
  <p>
    <strong>Result:</strong> I ran the program with no subject matter expert, a concurrent live campaign load, and creative with no prior send history. It did not succeed. I documented the authentication and reputation problems that resulted, and that failure is why I built the free <a href="/projects/ip-warming-checklist/">IP warming checklist</a> I wish I'd had.
  </p>

  <h3>Marketing wanted a lifecycle nurture program that could provide relevant product messaging to new prospects</h3>
  <p>
    <strong>Action:</strong> I engineered a lifecycle nurture that used first-party click behavior to route each prospect into the right specialization track. Engagement in the main branch determined which of five engagement programs a participant was routed toward, and action rules removed them from the general welcome program to prevent overlap. Sales reps regularly source their own leads, so I built static ad-hoc suppression and enrollment lists that let them decide whether specific prospects are added to or excluded from the journey.
  </p>
  <p>
    <strong>Result:</strong> In its first month live, with only two of five branches launched, the program cleared out a backlog of long-inactive prospects and generated new leads for sales. My pièce de résistance was taking the initiative to document the full program in a color-coded, bird's-eye flow diagram that explained the whole system to my colleagues.
  </p>
